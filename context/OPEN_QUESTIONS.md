# Context Open Questions

_Last updated: September 29, 2026_

These questions are intentionally unresolved. AI tools should challenge them rather than assume the current answer is correct.

## Product

- Is the enduring product truly a **Managed Webinar Channel**, or is webinar management the first delivery mechanism for a broader B2B acquisition / sales-activation system?
- How much of the operational webinar lifecycle should Context own?
- Which parts of the offer are differentiated IP versus necessary but commoditized fulfillment?
- Should the post-webinar enrichment, qualification, routing, and sales-handoff layer become a standalone product or capability?

## ICP

- Is $5M–$30M the right revenue band?
- Should Context only target firms already running webinars?
- Is accounting/tax advisory the long-term beachhead or simply the easiest place to establish proof?
- What organizational traits matter more than revenue: sales team structure, webinar cadence, marketing maturity, CRM maturity, average engagement value, or something else?

## Positioning

- Should the company lead with webinar management, revenue operations, sales enablement, demand activation, or another category?
- Is “Managed Webinar Channel” strong enough to communicate the differentiated value?
- Is the promise “an enriched, prioritized list of who to talk to after the event” large enough to support a $10K/month purchase, or should it ladder to qualified conversations and pipeline?
- How should Context explain the difference between this system and a webinar agency?

## Offer and conversion

- Is $10,000/month the correct packaging and price?
- Should the first engagement be one event, a pilot, or a multi-event program?
- Should every webinar sell a defined paid next step?
- How should free strategy / working sessions fit with the doctrine that the webinar should lead to a defined paid next step?
- What level of client participation is required for the program to work?

## Fulfillment

- Can recurring fulfillment actually be reduced from roughly 69 hours to 45 hours without reducing quality?
- Which tasks can be automated safely?
- Which judgment tasks must remain senior-led?
- How much of live production, creative, media buying, and project management should remain inside Context?
- What does the operating model look like for a new topic versus a recycled webinar topic?

## Economics

- What is actual customer acquisition cost?
- What is actual churn / retention?
- What contribution margin is achievable after fulfillment is proven?
- What media budget is actually required by ICP and topic?
- How should the model account for first-event setup costs versus recurring-event costs?

## Proof

- Which benchmark claims can be traced to raw campaign data?
- How stable are the current $10 CPR, 30% ICP-qualified, 30% attendance, and 3% booked-meeting benchmarks across clients and topics?
- What is booked-to-opportunity and opportunity-to-closed revenue performance?
- Does Tier 2 actually outperform Tier 3 in the sales-handoff model?
- What evidence would make the sales-intelligence layer defensible as proprietary IP?

## GTM

- What is the best first client acquisition motion: outbound, Karl's webinars, Ryan's network, partnerships, or some combination?
- Does targeting existing webinar programs create a strong enough observable trigger for outbound?
- What messaging produces replies from managing partners without sounding like a conventional lead-gen agency?
- What should the free / low-friction front-end strategy offer be?

## Partnership / roles

- What parts of the proposed Karl/Ryan role and compensation structure are actually agreed?
- At what client count should Ryan's work transition from hands-on strategy to oversight?
- How should strategic authority work once additional strategists are hired?
- Which business-development responsibilities belong to Karl versus Ryan?

## Rule for AI

Do not resolve these questions by assuming that the newest document must be correct.

When advising on them:
1. identify the current working hypothesis,
2. identify evidence supporting it,
3. identify evidence or logic against it,
4. recommend what should be tested or decided next.
